Historical records

Find the clues hidden in the archive.

Records can help place a person in time and community. The right sources depend on the question, location, period and what has survived.

Sources we may explore

Different records illuminate different lives.

Availability and access conditions vary. We identify suitable sources as part of agreeing the research scope.

R

Births, deaths and marriages

Registration indexes and certificates can establish family relationships and life events, subject to access rules.

C

Census and electoral records

Population records can offer glimpses of household, residence and occupation in particular years.

M

Migration and passenger lists

Passenger records, naturalisation files and migration sources may help trace journeys between countries.

N

Newspapers and directories

Local reporting and business or street directories can put people into their communities and working lives.

S

Military and service files

Service records may document enlistment, units, postings and other details, subject to privacy and archive conditions.

L

Land, probate and court

Property, wills and court records can provide evidence of relationships, assets and community connections.

P

Parish and church registers

Earlier baptisms, marriages and burials may survive where civil registration is absent or incomplete.

A

Archives and local histories

Institutional, local and family collections can add context that broad indexes do not capture.

Access and limitations

Not every record is online, open or conclusive.

Some records require in-person access, incur copy fees or are restricted to protect privacy. A result that appears to fit a person’s name must still be assessed against other evidence. We explain access limitations and uncertainties rather than treating an index match as proof.

Respectful handling

We follow relevant archive conditions and permissions. Please do not send private identity documents or sensitive information about living relatives with an initial enquiry.

Search with a question

A name is a start. Context narrows the search.

Approximate dates, places, family connections and an existing source can help focus research. If details are uncertain, say so — we can note them as clues rather than facts.
